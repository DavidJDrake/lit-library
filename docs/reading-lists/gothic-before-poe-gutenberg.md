# Gothic fiction before Poe, on Project Gutenberg

Survey of what Project Gutenberg holds (EPUB, US public domain) for the four authors epic 32
names as the Gothic tradition Poe inherited and Americanized: Horace Walpole, Ann Radcliffe,
Mary Shelley, and Charles Brockden Brown. Gutenberg ebook numbers below are from the Gutendex
catalog API, checked 2026-09-25; first-publication years are standard literary-reference dates
except where noted as verified this session.

Not yet in the library: none of these four authors appear in the current catalogue
(`out/catalog.json`, checked 2026-09-25) under any bundle.

## Horace Walpole — the invention of the form (2 books)

Walpole's Gothic output on Gutenberg is thin (most of his ~20 entries are political memoirs and
letters), but the one book that matters, matters completely.

| Title | Gutenberg ID | First published | Why it's here |
|---|---|---|---|
| The Castle of Otranto | 696 | 1764 | The first Gothic novel — the genre starts here |
| Hieroglyphic Tales | 14098 | 1785 | Walpole's own weird, fragmentary fantastical sketches |

**Needs a lookup, not invented:** *The Mysterious Mother* (1768), Walpole's incest-and-doom
Gothic tragedy, is the other major candidate — but I could not confirm a Gutenberg edition
credited to Walpole himself in this session's searches (only *The Count of Narbonne*, Robert
Jephson's stage adaptation of it, turned up, #36575). Check Gutenberg directly before assuming
it's absent.

**Left out on purpose:** the *Letters of Horace Walpole* (4 volumes), *Memoirs of the Reign of
King George the Second/Third* (7 volumes), and biographical works about him — none of it fiction.

## Ann Radcliffe — the genre's most-imitated voice (4 books)

Radcliffe is the Gothic novelist Austen parodied in *Northanger Abbey* and the one Poe most
visibly absorbed and reacted against — his reviews cite her directly, and "Radcliffean" was a
working adjective in his own critical vocabulary.

| Title | Gutenberg ID | First published | Why it's here |
|---|---|---|---|
| The Mysteries of Udolpho | 3268 | 1794 | The quintessential Gothic romance; the genre's best-known example |
| A Sicilian Romance | 7371 | 1790 | Early work; sets the persecuted-heroine, crumbling-castle template |
| The Romance of the Forest | 64701 | 1791 | Her breakthrough novel |
| The Italian | 74643, 74640 | 1797 | Her most controlled Gothic novel, a direct rival to Lewis's *The Monk* |

**Needs a lookup, not invented:** *The Italian* search results only turned up volumes 1 and 2
(of 3) — volume 3's Gutenberg ID needs a follow-up search before pulling. *Gaston de
Blondeville* (published posthumously, 1826), her last novel, also only partially turned up
(#78468, vol. 1 of 4) — confirm the full set exists before including it.

**Left out on purpose:** *A Journey Made in the Summer of 1794 ... through Holland and ... the
Rhine* (travel writing, not fiction) and French/Italian translations (*Les mystères d'Udolphe*,
*La forêt*, *I misteri del castello d'Udolfo*, *Les châteaux d'Athlin et de Dunbayne* — Radcliffe
in translation doesn't have the same "translator as author" case Baudelaire's Poe does; see the
decision memo in `poe-leftovers.md` for the reasoning that would apply here too).

## Mary Shelley — Gothic becomes something else (5 books)

Shelley is the hinge point between the Walpole/Radcliffe Gothic and the weird/SF tradition Poe
and later Lovecraft worked in — *Frankenstein* is a Gothic novel that mutates into something the
genre didn't have a name for yet.

| Title | Gutenberg ID | First published | Why it's here |
|---|---|---|---|
| Frankenstein; or, The Modern Prometheus | 84 | 1818 | The novel that turns Gothic dread into science fiction |
| The Last Man | 18247 | 1826 | Apocalyptic Gothic, underrated and rarely republished |
| Mathilda | 15238 | written 1819–20, published 1959 | Incest-and-suicide novella suppressed by her father, Gothic in the family-curse mode. Publication date verified via web search this session (Shelley Godwin Archive / Wikipedia); Gutenberg's release year is not the first-publication year |
| Valperga | 63337, 63338, 63339 | 1823 | Historical Gothic set in medieval Italy |
| Falkner | 64329 | 1837 | Her final novel; family secrets and a redemption plot in Gothic dress |

**Left out on purpose:** *Lodore* (1835, 3 vols, #64555–64557) and *The Fortunes of Perkin
Warbeck* (1830, #66749) — both closer to historical/domestic fiction than Gothic; *Proserpine
and Midas* (mythological verse dramas); *Tales and Stories* (a modern scholarly assembly, not a
period collection); her travel writing, biographical *Lives of the Most Eminent Literary and
Scientific Men*, and Percy Shelley editorial work.

## Charles Brockden Brown — the American Gothic Poe grew from (5 books)

Brown is the direct ancestor: an American writer building Radcliffean dread out of frontier
wilderness, religious fanaticism, and disease rather than castles, a full generation before Poe.

| Title | Gutenberg ID | First published | Why it's here |
|---|---|---|---|
| Wieland; or, The Transformation | 792 | 1798 | The template — ventriloquism, religious mania, a family destroyed from within |
| Edgar Huntly; or, Memoirs of a Sleep-Walker | 8223 | 1799 | Sleepwalking and the American wilderness as Gothic terrain |
| Arthur Mervyn; or, Memoirs of the Year 1793 | 18508 | 1799 | Yellow-fever Philadelphia as a Gothic landscape |
| Ormond; or, The Secret Witness | 36289, 36290, 36291 | 1799 | Conspiracy and surveillance Gothic |
| Memoirs of Carwin the Biloquist | 842 | 1803 (fragment; character originates in *Wieland*) | Unfinished prequel material — Brown's only other major fiction |

**Left out on purpose:** *Jane Talbot* (1801, #8404) — an epistolary domestic novel, not Gothic.
That's all 8 of Brown's Gutenberg fiction entries accounted for (the other 4 are volume splits
of *Ormond*); nothing was skipped for length, only for genre fit.
