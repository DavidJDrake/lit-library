# Alongside and after Poe, on Project Gutenberg

Survey of what Project Gutenberg holds (EPUB, US public domain) for the five authors epic 32
names as Poe's contemporaries and successors: Nathaniel Hawthorne, Washington Irving, Sheridan
Le Fanu, Bram Stoker, and Robert Louis Stevenson. Gutenberg ebook numbers below are from the
Gutendex catalog API, checked 2026-09-25; first-publication years are standard literary-reference
dates except where noted as verified this session.

Not yet in the library: none of these five authors appear in the current catalogue
(`out/catalog.json`, checked 2026-09-25) under any bundle. (Irving predates Poe in age but is
listed here per BACKLOG.md's grouping, since "The Legend of Sleepy Hollow" and "The Adventure of
the German Student" sit stylistically alongside Poe's tales rather than in the Walpole/Radcliffe
Gothic lineage.)

## Nathaniel Hawthorne — the writer Poe reviewed (7 books)

Poe's 1842 review of *Twice-Told Tales* is where he laid out his theory of the short story's
"single effect" — Hawthorne is the contemporary Poe used to define his own aesthetic, admiringly
and combatively at once.

| Title | Gutenberg ID | First published | Why it's here |
|---|---|---|---|
| Twice-Told Tales | 508 | 1837 | The collection Poe reviewed; the direct link between the two writers |
| The Snow-Image, and Other Twice-Told Tales | 513 | 1852 | Companion collection, same mode |
| Mosses from an Old Manse | 512 | 1846 | "Young Goodman Brown," "The Minister's Black Veil" — Hawthorne's darkest short fiction |
| The Scarlet Letter | 33 | 1850 | His masterpiece; Puritan guilt as Gothic machinery |
| The House of the Seven Gables | 77 | 1851 | A family curse across generations, explicitly Gothic |
| The Blithedale Romance | 2081 | 1852 | Utopian-community novel with a Gothic drowning at its center |
| The Marble Faun | 2181, 2182 | 1860 | His last novel; guilt and transformation in Rome |

**Left out on purpose:** *A Wonder Book for Girls & Boys* and *Tanglewood Tales* (children's
myth retellings — a different register entirely), *True Stories of History and Biography*, *The
Whole History of Grandfather's Chair*, *Our Old Home* and the *Notebooks* (travel writing and
journals), *Doctor Grimshawe's Secret* (unfinished, published posthumously from fragmentary
drafts), and duplicate/foreign editions (#25344, #36990 Spanish, #55215 Spanish).

## Washington Irving — the American tale before Poe had rivals (5 books)

Irving's *Sketch Book* predates Poe's career and gave American short fiction its first
internationally read Gothic-adjacent tales; "The Adventure of the German Student" in *Tales of a
Traveller* is a genuine horror story that anticipates Poe's own.

| Title | Gutenberg ID | First published | Why it's here |
|---|---|---|---|
| The Sketch Book of Geoffrey Crayon | 2048 | 1819–1820 | Contains "Rip Van Winkle" and "The Legend of Sleepy Hollow" |
| Tales of a Traveller | 13514 | 1824 | Contains "The Adventure of the German Student," a real horror tale |
| Bracebridge Hall | 13515 | 1822 | Companion collection to the Sketch Book, English-set |
| The Alhambra | 49947 | 1832 | Moorish legends, atmospheric and ruin-haunted |
| Wolfert's Roost, and Miscellanies | 8571 | 1855 | Late collection, more Hudson Valley legend material |

**Left out on purpose:** standalone "Rip Van Winkle" and "The Legend of Sleepy Hollow" excerpts
(#41, #60976, #23965, #64636 — redundant with the *Sketch Book*), *Knickerbocker's History of
New York* (satire, not Gothic-adjacent), *Old Christmas* and *Little Britain* (Sketch Book
excerpts republished separately), *Salmagundi* (early satirical periodical work), and Irving's
substantial non-fiction output (*Life of George Washington*, *Life and Voyages of Christopher
Columbus*, *Astoria*, *The Adventures of Captain Bonneville*, *Chronicle of the Conquest of
Granada*, *Oliver Goldsmith: A Biography*, *Spanish Papers*, *The Crayon Papers*).

## Sheridan Le Fanu — the ghost story between Poe and Lovecraft (7 books)

Le Fanu is the bridge the Lovecraft-circle document already documents on the other side (M. R.
James's admitted model); on this side, he's Poe's closest Anglo-Irish successor in the
psychological ghost story, and *Carmilla* predates *Dracula* by a quarter-century.

| Title | Gutenberg ID | First published | Why it's here |
|---|---|---|---|
| Uncle Silas: A Tale of Bartram-Haugh | 14851 | 1864 | Le Fanu's masterpiece; a locked-house suspense Gothic |
| Carmilla | 10007 | 1872 | The foundational lesbian vampire novella, standalone edition |
| In a Glass Darkly | 37172, 37173, 37174 | 1872 | The collection *Carmilla* was first published in, plus "Green Tea" and "Mr Justice Harbottle" |
| The House by the Church-Yard | 17769 | 1863 | Small-town Gothic mystery, an influence on James Joyce |
| Wylder's Hand | 9983 | 1864 | Sensation-Gothic hybrid, closer to Collins than to horror |
| The Purcell Papers | 509, 510, 511 | stories 1838–1840, collected 1880 | Le Fanu's earliest supernatural tales, magazine-published in Poe's own decade. Dates verified via web search this session |
| The Watcher, and Other Weird Stories | 40510 | — (a modern selection; individual story dates vary, not verified this session) | A commonly anthologized selection of his shorter weird fiction |

**Left out on purpose:** roughly 25 further titles — the rest of his sensation novels
(*Checkmate*, *Guy Deverell*, *The Tenants of Malory*, *The Cock and Anchor*, *Willing to Die*,
*All in the Dark*, *The Evil Guest*, *The Wyvern Mystery*, *The Room in the Dragon Volant*, *A
Stable for Nightmares*), and duplicate/alternate-cut story collections (the five-volume "J. S.
Le Fanu's Ghostly Tales" series, which overlaps heavily with *In a Glass Darkly* and *The
Purcell Papers*).

## Bram Stoker — the vampire novel's culmination (6 books)

*Dracula* is the destination this whole direction points toward — the Gothic vampire tradition
Le Fanu, Polidori, and folklore built, brought to its most famous form.

| Title | Gutenberg ID | First published | Why it's here |
|---|---|---|---|
| Dracula | 345 | 1897 | Unavoidable — the genre's definitive vampire novel |
| Dracula's Guest | 10150 | 1914 (posthumous) | The deleted opening chapter of *Dracula*, plus other stories |
| The Snake's Pass | 68966 | 1890 | Stoker's first novel, Irish Gothic before *Dracula* |
| The Jewel of Seven Stars | 3781 | 1903 | Egyptian-curse horror, heavily influential on the mummy-horror subgenre |
| The Lady of the Shroud | 3095 | 1909 | Vampire-adjacent political thriller |
| The Lair of the White Worm | 1188 | 1911 | His last novel, uneven but a genuine influence on later pulp horror |

**Left out on purpose:** *The Mystery of the Sea* (1902, occult thriller — thinner than the
above), *The Man* (romance, not horror), *Famous Impostors* and *Personal Reminiscences of
Henry Irving* (non-fiction), *Lady Athlyne* (straight romance, no horror content).

## Robert Louis Stevenson — split selves and Gothic ambiguity (6 books)

Stevenson brings the Gothic double back into literary fiction with *Jekyll and Hyde*, and his
short weird fiction (Markheim's devil, the burked corpses of "The Body Snatcher"-adjacent
tales) sits squarely in Poe's register even though Stevenson is best known for adventure fiction.

| Title | Gutenberg ID | First published | Why it's here |
|---|---|---|---|
| Strange Case of Dr Jekyll and Mr Hyde | 43 | 1886 | The defining split-self horror novella |
| The Merry Men, and Other Tales and Fables | 344 | 1887 (collection); includes "Markheim" (1885) and "Thrawn Janet" (1881) | Stevenson's core horror/weird shorts in one volume |
| New Arabian Nights | 839 | 1882 | Includes "The Suicide Club" — proto-weird crime fiction |
| The Master of Ballantrae | 864 | 1889 | Family-curse Gothic with a supernatural-tinged ending |
| Weir of Hermiston | 380 | 1896 (unfinished, published posthumously) | Dark Scots tragedy, considered his best unfinished work |
| The Ebb-Tide | 1604 | 1894 (with Lloyd Osbourne) | South Seas Gothic, moral rot at the edge of empire |

**Left out on purpose:** *Treasure Island* and *Kidnapped* (adventure fiction, no Gothic or
weird register), *A Child's Garden of Verses* (poetry for children), *Catriona* (adventure
sequel to *Kidnapped*), his travel writing and essay collections (*Travels with a Donkey*, *In
the South Seas*, *Virginibus Puerisque*, *Familiar Studies of Men and Books*, *Essays in the
Art of Writing*), and his letters.
