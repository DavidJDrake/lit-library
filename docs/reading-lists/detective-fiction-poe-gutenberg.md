# Detective fiction Poe founded, on Project Gutenberg

Survey of what Project Gutenberg holds (EPUB, US public domain) for the three authors epic 32
names as the direct line from Poe's Dupin stories to the classic detective novel: Arthur Conan
Doyle, Wilkie Collins, and Anna Katharine Green. Gutenberg ebook numbers below are from the
Gutendex catalog API, checked 2026-09-25; first-publication years are standard literary-reference
dates. Years I could not independently confirm this session are marked "unverified."

Not yet in the library: none of these three authors appear in the current catalogue
(`out/catalog.json`, checked 2026-09-25) under any bundle, so there's no duplicate risk.

## Arthur Conan Doyle — the Holmes canon (9 books)

Holmes is Dupin's most direct descendant — Doyle said as much, and Watson narrating a genius
he can't quite keep up with is the Dupin/narrator pair formalized into a franchise. Because the
canon itself is the point of this entry, I'm taking all nine Holmes books rather than a subset;
what's left out is everything Doyle wrote that isn't Holmes.

| Title | Gutenberg ID | First published | Why it's here |
|---|---|---|---|
| A Study in Scarlet | 244 | 1887 | Holmes and Watson's introduction; the method stated outright |
| The Adventures of Sherlock Holmes | 1661 | 1892 | The stories that made the character — "A Scandal in Bohemia," "The Red-Headed League" |
| The Sign of the Four | 2097 | 1890 | Second novel; the deduction set-pieces mature |
| The Memoirs of Sherlock Holmes | 834 | 1894 | Includes "The Final Problem" (Reichenbach Falls) |
| The Return of Sherlock Holmes | 108 | 1905 | Includes "The Adventure of the Empty House," undoing the above |
| The Hound of the Baskervilles | 2852 | 1902 | The best-known novel — Gothic moor atmosphere solved by ratiocination |
| His Last Bow | 2350 | 1917 | Wartime spy story; Holmes brought into the 20th century |
| The Valley of Fear | 3289 | 1915 | Last novel; a Pinkerton-agent backstory structured like a locked-room case |
| The Case-Book of Sherlock Holmes | 69700 | 1927 | Final collection, weaker but closes the canon |

**Left out on purpose:** duplicate/illustrated Gutenberg editions of the above (e.g. #28733,
#9551, #9555, #9556, #21520, #21586, #3070, #9552, #9553 — same texts, different scans);
foreign-language editions (Finnish, French); *The Lost World* and the Professor Challenger
books (science fiction, not detective); *The White Company* and *Sir Nigel* (historical
adventure); *Round the Red Lamp* (#423 — medical horror stories, closer to weird fiction than
detection); *Memories and Adventures* and *The Great Boer War* (non-fiction).

## Wilkie Collins — the sensation novels (5 books)

Collins predates Doyle and, with Poe, is usually credited alongside him as inventing detective
fiction's English branch — *The Moonstone* is often called the first English detective novel.
His wider "sensation novel" output blends Gothic dread with the procedural unpacking of a
crime, which is exactly this direction's hinge.

| Title | Gutenberg ID | First published | Why it's here |
|---|---|---|---|
| The Moonstone | 155 | 1868 | Sergeant Cuff, the diamond, the first English detective novel |
| The Woman in White | 583 | 1859 | The sensation-novel template; Walter Hartright investigates before there's a word for it |
| Armadale | 1895 | 1866 | Collins's most tightly plotted sensation novel, built around a forged identity |
| No Name | 1438 | 1862 | A woman investigating and forging her own case against her family |
| The Law and the Lady | 1622 | 1875 | A female amateur detective (Valeria Woodville) reopening a closed case |

**Left out on purpose:** *The Haunted Hotel* (#170, minor mystery/supernatural hybrid), *The
Dead Secret* (#43092), and the rest of the ~39 Gutenberg entries — melodramas and domestic
novels (*Basil*, *Poor Miss Finch*, *Man and Wife*, *The Black Robe*, *Heart and Science*, *The
Evil Genius*, *The New Magdalen*, *"I Say No"*, *The Legacy of Cain*, *My Lady's Money*, *The
Two Destinies*, *A Rogue's Life*, *The Fallen Leaves*, *Miss or Mrs.?*, *Hide and Seek*, *Little
Novels*), Collins's Dickens collaborations (*No Thoroughfare*, *The Lazy Tour of Two Idle
Apprentices*, *A House to Let*), and one travel book (*Rambles Beyond Railways*).

## Anna Katharine Green — the American procedural (10 books)

Green is the direction's least-known name but arguably its most important: an American woman
writing systematic, clue-driven detective novels a decade before Doyle, with a series
detective (Ebenezer Gryce) and, later, one of the first female series detectives written by a
woman (Violet Strange).

| Title | Gutenberg ID | First published | Why it's here |
|---|---|---|---|
| The Leavenworth Case | 4047 | 1878 | Her first novel; established the form and made her a bestseller |
| Hand and Ring | 31681 | 1883 | Early Gryce case, tightly clued |
| That Affair Next Door | 21617 | 1897 | Introduces Amelia Butterworth, a nosy-neighbor amateur detective paired with Gryce |
| Lost Man's Lane | 33305 | 1898 | Second Butterworth novel |
| The Circular Study | 18761 | 1900 | Third and final Gryce/Butterworth pairing |
| Agatha Webb | 5162 | 1899 | Standalone, considered one of her tightest plots |
| The Filigree Ball | 2371 | 1903 | Washington-set mystery, a favorite among her later novels |
| One of My Sons | 34710 | 1901 | Family-secrets mystery, Gryce in a supporting role |
| Dark Hollow | 5121 | 1914 | Later Gryce novel, courtroom-heavy |
| The Golden Slipper, and Other Problems for Violet Strange | 3071 | 1915 | Introduces Violet Strange, a debutante consulting detective — story collection |

**Left out on purpose:** roughly 30 further titles, including *Initials Only* and *The Mystery
of the Hasty Arrow* (more Violet Strange, diminishing returns), *X Y Z* and *7 to 12* (short
detective-story pamphlets), *The House of the Whispering Pines*, *Cynthia Wakeham's Money*,
*The Mill Mystery*, *The Millionaire Baby*, *Doctor Izard*, *The Sword of Damocles*, *The
Forsaken Inn*, *A Strange Disappearance*, *The Woman in the Alcove*, *The Mayor's Wife*, *Room
Number 3*, *Three Thousand Dollars*, *The Staircase at the Heart's Delight*, *The Step on the
Stair*, *The Doctor, His Wife, and the Clock*, *The Old Stone House*, and the multi-author
anthology *Eleven Possible Cases*.
