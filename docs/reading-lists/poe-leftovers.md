# Poe leftovers: anthologies and translations

Decision memo for the two groups epic 32 deliberately left out of the 2026-09-13 Edgar Allan
Poe by Project Gutenberg pull (22 books, in the library): 17 anthologies/periodicals that credit
Poe among other writers, and 17 translations. Both groups turned up in the original Gutendex
survey for "Edgar Allan Poe" but weren't pulled because neither is straightforwardly "a Poe
book" the way the 22 already in the library are.

I did not re-derive the exact 17+17 lists this session — that count comes from the person who
did the original Poe pull and I'm treating it as given. What follows is a recommendation on how
to treat each group *if* a batch is built from them, not a re-verified inventory. Before pulling
either group, re-run the Gutendex survey to get the current, complete, exact list.

## The anthologies and periodicals (17)

**What they are:** multi-author collections and magazine issues where Poe is one credited
contributor among several — named examples from BACKLOG.md are *The Lock and Key Library* and
*Famous Modern Ghost Stories*, plus eight 1836 issues of the *Southern Literary Messenger*, the
magazine Poe edited from December 1835 into 1837. (I confirmed via Gutendex this session that
Gutenberg holds 26 *Southern Literary Messenger* issues total, spanning Vol. I No. 1, August
1834 through Vol. II No. 12, November 1836 — all credited to "Various," not to Poe individually.)

**How they'd sit in the catalogue:** the site's catalog cards group by title and author. A
*Southern Literary Messenger* issue's author is "Various" — it would not file under "Edgar Allan
Poe" at all unless the pull manually overrode authorship, which would misrepresent a magazine
issue as a Poe book. The general-interest anthologies (*Lock and Key Library*, *Famous Modern
Ghost Stories*) have the same problem one level down: Poe is one name in a contents list the
catalogue can't see into, alongside authors who may have nothing to do with the fiction shelves
this epic is building (detective, Gothic, or weird).

**Recommendation: leave them out**, with one narrow exception worth a human decision.

- The *Southern Literary Messenger* issues are a research/reference asset (Poe's own editorial
  work, the magazine that first ran some of his fiction and criticism) more than a reading-shelf
  asset. They'd clutter both a "Poe" shelf (he's not really the author) and a "Fiction" shelf
  (each issue is mixed poetry, fiction, essays, and reviews from many hands). If they're wanted
  at all, they belong as a separate small bundle — something like *Southern Literary Messenger
  (Project Gutenberg)*, category "Other" or a research-materials category if one exists, not
  filed as Poe's — and only if a reader has actually asked for this kind of primary-source
  material. Nothing in epic 31 (reader engagement) suggests anyone has.
- The general anthologies (*Lock and Key Library*, *Famous Modern Ghost Stories*, and whatever
  else makes up the 17) are a better fit for the detective and Gothic/weird shelves this epic is
  already building than for a Poe shelf — an anthology titled "Famous Modern Ghost Stories" with
  Poe as one contributor is more useful sitting near Bierce, Le Fanu, and M. R. James collections
  than filed under Poe. If any of them turn out to be genuinely well-regarded, era-defining
  anthologies (worth checking table-of-contents by table-of-contents), they could be pulled as
  standalone titles credited to "Various" with a description noting which authors are inside —
  the same pattern the *Southern Literary Messenger* would need. Otherwise, skip them; a shelf
  built from anthology grab-bags reads worse than one built from single-author, single-work books.

## The translations (17)

**What they are:** non-English editions of Poe's work. The one I confirmed this session:
*Histoires extraordinaires* (Gutenberg #20761), Charles Baudelaire's 1856 French translation —
the collection that made Poe a major literary reputation in France and directly influenced
Mallarmé and the French Symbolists. BACKLOG.md's framing (Baudelaire's versions are "literary
works in their own right") is accurate: this isn't a mechanical translation, it's a canonical
text of French literature that happens to be a translation of an American one.

**How they'd sit in the catalogue:** this is the harder case, because the catalogue groups by
title *and author* — if "Histoires extraordinaires" files under "Edgar Allan Poe," a reader
browsing the Poe shelf for English text gets a French book with no warning; if it files under
"Baudelaire," it's arguably miscategorized the other way, since Baudelaire didn't write the
stories. Neither default is right without a description that says plainly what the book is.

**Recommendation: take the Baudelaire volumes specifically; skip the rest by default.**

- *Histoires extraordinaires* (and its follow-up volume *Nouvelles histoires extraordinaires*,
  if Gutenberg has it — not confirmed this session, needs a lookup) are worth adding as their
  own small bundle, titled and credited so the translation relationship is explicit up front:
  something like "Histoires extraordinaires (trans. Charles Baudelaire)" with authors listed as
  both Poe and Baudelaire, and a description that says outright "Baudelaire's 1856 French
  translation, itself a landmark of French literature — not an English text." That's honest
  cataloguing rather than filing a French book as if it were one of the 22 English Poe titles
  already in the library.
- The other translations (German, Spanish, Finnish, and whatever else makes up the remaining
  ~15) don't have Baudelaire's case for literary standing in their own right — they're ordinary
  translations, useful to a reader of that language but not adding anything a fiction-in-English
  library is organized around. Leave them out. If the library ever adds a non-English shelf,
  they'd be candidates then; until it does, they're clutter on the Poe shelf with the same
  "silent foreign-language book" problem as the anthologies above, and with no compensating
  literary-historical reason the way Baudelaire has.

## Summary

| Group | Recommendation | If taken, how |
|---|---|---|
| 8 Southern Literary Messenger issues (1836) | Leave out | Separate "Southern Literary Messenger" bundle, credited "Various," not filed as Poe |
| Remaining ~9 general anthologies | Leave out by default | Only pull individually-vetted ones, credited "Various," described by contents |
| Baudelaire's French Poe translations | Take | Own small bundle, both Poe and Baudelaire credited, description states it's a French translation |
| Remaining ~15 translations (other languages) | Leave out | No case for literary standing beyond ordinary translation; revisit only if a non-English shelf is ever built |

Nothing here has been pulled or published — this is a recommendation for a human to accept,
adjust, or reject before any of it becomes a BACKLOG.md checkbox getting ticked.
